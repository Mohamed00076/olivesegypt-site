What Drives the Price per KG

Table-olive prices move with the season, so we don't publish a fixed price list — any number printed today could be wrong by the time you read it. Here is what actually drives the price, and how to get a current, honest quote.

  • Harvest yield & weather. Egypt's main olive harvest runs through the autumn. A strong yield increases supply and softens prices into the new season; drought or an off-cycle year (olive trees follow a natural biennial bearing pattern) tightens availability.
  • Currency movements. Exports are priced in USD, so the Egyptian pound's exchange rate affects competitiveness. Currency volatility introduces short-term swings worth watching if you're timing an order.
  • Variety & caliber. Large-caliber green olives and stuffed specialty lines sit at the premium end. Mid-caliber and oxidized black olives offer better value for high-volume food service.
  • Packaging & freight. Glass costs more per unit than bulk barrel; ocean freight rates and container availability also feed into your landed cost even when the FOB price hasn't moved.

An illustrative example (not a quote): consider a buyer sourcing Aggizi Green Olives, caliber 141/160, in bulk barrel packaging, for a full 20ft container FOB Alexandria. Their landed cost per kg would reflect the current FOB price for that caliber and packaging, ocean freight to their port, import duty in their market, and any local repacking cost. Illustrative only — not a quote or a binding offer. Actual FOB pricing depends on the current season and is confirmed on request.

Requesting a current quote: tell us the variety and caliber you need, your preferred packaging format, your target order volume, your destination port, and which Incoterm you prefer (FOB, CIF, or CFR). We'll confirm a current, honest price against exactly those terms rather than a generic range.

Packaging Formats We Offer

  • Glass Jars. Premium retail and deli presentation. Common sizes from 300g to 1.7kg. Showcases the product; supports branded and private-label labeling.
  • Tin Cans. Shelf-stable and durable, ideal for food service, warm-climate export, and oxidized black olive lines. Stack and ship efficiently.
  • Plastic Buckets. Food-grade buckets, typically 1–10kg, for restaurants, caterers, and repackers. Balance cost, volume, and convenience.
  • Wooden / Plastic Barrels. Bulk brine maturation and shipping, typically 50–200kg, for the lowest cost per kilogram on large volumes.
  • Vacuum Pouches. Compact, lightweight format for pitted or sliced product where brine weight and volume need to be minimized.

These are the packaging formats confirmed as available. Exact sizes, case configurations, and minimum order quantities per format are confirmed during quotation and depend on the specific product and order volume.

Understanding Caliber Sizing

Table olives are graded by caliber — the count of olives per kilogram, written as a range such as 181/200. A lower number means larger individual olives. Premium retail jars typically use 101/110 to 181/200; food-service and bulk repacking often use 201/230 and above at a lower cost per kilogram. Exact caliber ranges available for each variety are listed on the product catalog and each product's own page.
