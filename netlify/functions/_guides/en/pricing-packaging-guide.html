What Drives the Price per KG

Table-olive prices move with the season, so we don't publish a fixed price list — any number printed today could be wrong by the time you read it. Here is what actually drives the price, and how to get a current, honest quote.

  • Harvest yield & weather. Egypt's main olive harvest runs through the autumn. A strong yield increases supply and softens prices into the new season; drought or an off-cycle year (olive trees follow a natural biennial bearing pattern) tightens availability.
  • Currency movements. Quotes and payment can be in USD or EUR. Movements in the Egyptian pound's exchange rate can affect prices between quotations. Currency volatility introduces short-term swings worth watching if you're timing an order.
  • Variety & caliber. Price varies by product, caliber, packaging and order volume, and is confirmed in the quotation.
  • Packaging & freight. Glass costs more per unit than bulk barrel; ocean freight rates and container availability also feed into your landed cost even when the FOB price hasn't moved.

An illustrative example (not a quote): consider a buyer sourcing Green Olives, caliber 141/160, in bulk barrel packaging, for a full 20ft container FOB Alexandria. Their landed cost per kg would reflect the current FOB price for that caliber and packaging, ocean freight to their port, import duty in their market, and any local repacking cost. Illustrative only — not a quote or a binding offer. Actual FOB pricing depends on the current season and is confirmed on request.

Understanding Caliber Sizing

Table olives are graded by caliber — the count of olives per kilogram, written as a range such as 181/200. A lower number means larger individual olives. We supply calibers from 140 to 360; the caliber for your order is confirmed in the quotation. Exact caliber ranges available for each variety are listed on the product catalog and each product's own page.
